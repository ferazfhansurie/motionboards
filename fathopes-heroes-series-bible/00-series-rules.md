# Series Rules

## Non-Negotiables

1. The world is real Malaysia.
   Real streets, mamak shops, food courts, wet markets, depots, mini-tankers, drains, back alleys, event booths, and FatHopes facilities.

2. The heroes are 2D animated characters inside real locations.
   They should feel obviously animated, but naturally staged in the camera frame.

3. FatHopes is helpful, not preachy.
   The heroes solve problems, explain simply, and make vendors feel smart for doing the right thing.

4. The tone is funny-serious.
   The mission matters, but the scenes are grounded in awkward Malaysian daily life.

5. Every episode needs one practical takeaway.
   The audience should know what to do after watching.

6. Keep the UCO details specific.
   Use drums, tong minyak, funnels, grease traps, route timing, collection app, litre estimates, bad smell, slippery floors, contaminated oil, restaurant closing hours, cash/value, SAF, and drain pollution.

7. Avoid generic superhero speeches.
   No vague lines like "Together we save the planet" unless someone immediately undercuts it with a practical joke.

8. Do not over-Americanise the dialogue.
   This is Klang Valley / Malaysia energy, not Marvel banter.

9. Do not make vendors look stupid.
   Vendors are busy, practical, and sometimes misinformed. The heroes respect them.

10. Do not make the heroes perfect.
   They are competent, but each has a weakness that creates comedy.

## Core Mythology

The FatHopes Superheroes are eco-warriors fighting pollution, reclaiming resources, and helping used cooking oil become sustainable biofuel.

Their enemy is Geng Orang Minyak (G.O.M.), a shadowy syndicate in Fataysia. G.O.M. spreads careless disposal, contaminates usable resources, exploits low awareness, and profits when communities treat used cooking oil like rubbish.

G.O.M. villains should not always steal. Often, their real weapon is sabotage: one splash of water, one fake shortcut, one careless rumour, one contaminated tong, one vendor convinced that proper collection is too troublesome.

## G.O.M. Motive

G.O.M. profits when used cooking oil stays messy, untracked, and misunderstood.

If vendors believe UCO is rubbish, they dump it, mix it, hide it, or pass it to shady collectors. That chaos gives G.O.M. power.

FatHopes threatens G.O.M. because it turns waste into accountable value:

- vendors learn to store oil cleanly
- collections become trackable
- dirty shortcuts become less attractive
- pollution becomes harder to hide
- clean oil can move toward sustainable fuel pathways

So G.O.M.'s goal is not only to steal oil. Their bigger goal is to break trust in the clean system.

Their best win is psychological:

> Make vendors think proper collection is troublesome, risky, or pointless.

## Episode Length Rules

For TikTok/Reels:

- 0-3 seconds: visual hook or absurd problem.
- 3-10 seconds: reveal the UCO issue.
- 10-35 seconds: heroes respond, conflict escalates.
- 35-60 seconds: solution and punchline.
- 60 seconds onward: only if the scene has strong character comedy.

## What Makes A Good FatHopes Heroes Episode

A good episode has:

- A mundane real-world UCO problem.
- A superhero-level overreaction.
- A specific Malaysian location.
- One clear character conflict.
- One educational point.
- One final joke or button.

Bad episode idea:

> The heroes teach people to recycle oil.

Good episode idea:

> A warung uncle uses an old Milo tin to store oil, labels it "jangan kacau", and Kit treats it like a field hazard while Sparron calmly scans it and says, "Actually, this one can become fuel." Meanwhile Glinciro tries to convince the uncle that dumping it would be "more efficient."
