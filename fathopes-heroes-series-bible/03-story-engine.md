# Story Engine

## Series Promise

Every episode turns one ordinary used-cooking-oil problem into a tiny superhero mission.

The audience comes for the ridiculous hero response, stays for the character chemistry, and leaves knowing what FatHopes does.

## The Episode Formula

1. The Ordinary Problem
   A real Malaysian UCO issue appears.

2. The Hero Misread
   One hero treats it too dramatically or too casually.

3. The Practical Complication
   A vendor, route issue, app issue, contaminated oil, or timing problem makes it harder.

4. The Team Dynamic
   The heroes disagree because their personalities clash.

5. The FatHopes Solution
   Someone explains the real fix simply.

6. The Button Joke
   End on a funny consequence, not a corporate slogan.

## The Aha Rule

Every episode needs one click moment where the audience suddenly understands the real point through the comedy.

The episode should not start by explaining the lesson. It should start with a funny wrong assumption, escalate that wrong assumption, then reveal the truth.

Good aha shape:

1. Character thinks the problem is simple.
2. Shortcut makes it weird.
3. Weirdness escalates visually.
4. Someone notices the real pattern.
5. One simple line makes it click.
6. The practical FatHopes solution becomes obvious.

Example:

Wrong assumption:
"If I pour oil into the drain, it disappears."

Aha:
"It did not disappear. It became tomorrow's bill."

Practical solution:
Store it clean and request collection.

## Strong Episode Sources

Use these for episode premises:

- Vendor pours used oil into drain.
- Oil is mixed with water, sambal, or food scraps.
- A vendor thinks used oil has no value.
- Someone stores oil in a dangerous container.
- Driver arrives during peak lunch chaos.
- The app request is made wrong.
- The route has too many pickups and one hero insists on "saving the schedule."
- A customer slips near oily floor.
- Illegal collector offers fast cash.
- Hero tries to measure oil with ridiculous seriousness.
- Hospital/school/community booth collects oil and nobody understands why.
- Mini-tanker gets treated like a superhero vehicle.

## Character Conflict Matrix

### Dr.UP vs Kit

Talk vs labour.

Example:
Dr.UP gives a heroic speech while Kit is physically carrying the oil drum behind him.

### Sparron vs Kit

Strategy vs field reality.

Example:
Sparron says the app flow is simple. Kit points at the vendor's cracked phone screen.

### Dr.UP vs Reality

Big heroic framing vs messy operations.

Example:
Dr.UP announces the mission is under control, then immediately realises nobody brought the funnel.

### Glinciro vs The Team

Stylish sabotage vs practical responsibility.

Example:
Glinciro convinces a vendor to dump oil fast. The team has to prove proper collection is easier and more valuable long term.

## Episode Quality Test

Before writing, answer:

- What is the specific UCO problem?
- Why is it funny visually?
- Which hero is wrong at first?
- Which hero solves it?
- What does the audience learn?
- What is the last joke?

If you cannot answer all six, the episode is not ready.

## Avoid These

- Generic climate lectures.
- Villains who only say "I hate recycling."
- Random action scenes without UCO logic.
- Everyone speaking the same Manglish.
- Too many facts in one episode.
- Making FatHopes look like a charity only. It is practical value, collection, app, routes, and real impact.
